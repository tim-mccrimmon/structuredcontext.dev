## SCS v0.6.0 (Previous)

Released 2026-10-19 and superseded by [v0.7.0](/docs/specification/v0-7/).

**Theme:** governance context that a runtime can deliver as-is.

:::caution[Breaking change from 0.5.0]
If you have 0.5.0 content, see the [0.5 → 0.6.0 migration guide](/docs/specification/migration-0-6/).
:::

## What was new

- **Two independent governance bundles.** A **Domain bundle** holds an organization's
  governance. A **Project bundle** holds one project's governance. A Project bundle no longer
  imports the Domain bundle. The two are compiled separately and applied side by side, so
  neither has to be re-released when the other changes.
- **Standards became citations.** Standards bundles and the standards tier were
  deprecated. A standard now appears only as a [citation](/docs/specification/citations/),
  such as `std:iso-13485:2016#7.3.9`, and SCS never carries standard text.
- **The runtime artifact and `scs compile`.** A Domain or Project bundle compiles to one
  small, deterministic file of prohibitions, directives, and facts, each with a stable ID. See
  [Runtime Artifact](/docs/specification/runtime-artifact/).
- **The meta bundle is published with the specification** and ships inside
  `scs-validator`.
- **The Project tier, clarified.** Organization-wide rules are `scd:project:*` SCDs too. The
  tier identifies who authors an SCD, not where it applies.
- **Tooling.** `scs new domain`, `--ontology cdmo` scaffolding, and validation that resolves
  the whole bundle tree.

## Why standards became citations

The text of most standards is licensed to the adopter, not to the specification or a
vendor. And the authoritative interpretation of a standard is the adopter's own quality
system. SCS holds that interpretation as rules and keeps the standard as the reference. Where
an adopter wants AI systems to consult a standard's full text, that belongs in retrieval, not
in governance.
